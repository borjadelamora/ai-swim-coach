# Turns & walls — flip turn, timing cues, push-off, wall-skill sets

Reference for the Coach when turn or wall work is on the menu. GENERIC method — the
swimmer's current turn status/limiter lives in the profile and wins on conflict.

## The flip turn in four phases — coach ONE per session
1. APPROACH — maintain speed into the wall; do NOT breathe on the last stroke (lifting
   the head drops the hips and rushes everything); do NOT decelerate. Judge distance
   from a calibrated cue (below), not hope. Begin the flip when fingertips are about
   half an arm's length from the wall.
2. SOMERSAULT — core-driven, never arm-driven. Tuck tight (chin to chest, heels to
   hips); a loose open tuck is slow and tiring. Drive head to knees. Land feet flat on
   the wall, toes up, knees bent ~90°.
3. PUSH-OFF — leave on the back in a locked streamline; arms snap to streamline BEFORE
   the feet leave the wall, body follows the arms. Rotate to front during the glide via
   core + kick. Depth: just under the surface turbulence — too deep wastes the push.
4. BREAKOUT — hold streamline, add underwater kicks, let speed carry into the first
   stroke; don't rush the first pull; delay the first breath until after the breakout
   (breath-underwater.md owns the hold/kick detail).

## Timing cues — judging the wall without guessing
The approach lives or dies on a repeatable distance cue. Build a PRIMARY + BACKUP cue
per pool; when the pool changes, the cue must be recalibrated before turn quality is
judged at all.
- Floor markings (the T / lane-line end): the classic — time the last breath/stroke to
  land AT the mark. Only works where markings exist.
- WALL SIGHTING (works in any pool): eyes open, glance forward through the last strokes;
  the wall resolves sharply at a stable distance in clear water. Calibrate at easy pace:
  note where detail on the wall becomes readable, count strokes from there to the turn;
  repeat until the count is stable, then trust the count at speed.
- STROKE COUNT per length: strokes/length is stable at a given pace once counted over
  several lengths. Count from push-off (or from a fixed mid-pool feature); the last 2-3
  strokes are swum by number, breath placed to finish the cycle before the flip. Recount
  when pace or pool changes — count scales with both.
- Overhead references: backstroke flags, a rope shadow, a ceiling/beam line (indoor), a
  ladder or lane-rope color band near the wall — anything fixed works if calibrated the
  same way: find where it passes overhead/level with you, count strokes from it.
- Calibration ramp (same for any cue): groove at easy pace until ~9/10 consistent →
  ramp approach speed 50→70→85→100% keeping stroke rhythm all the way in → only then
  judge turn quality under fatigue. A cue that works fresh but not tired isn't
  calibrated yet — re-groove at moderate pace before re-testing tired.

## Drill progression (lightest → hardest)
Mid-pool somersaults (no wall) → stationary wall plant (feet placement, push posture)
→ slow approach with full mechanics → speed ramp with the calibrated cue → race-pace
entries keeping stroke rhythm → tired-wall work inside normal sets. Enter this ladder
at the current limiter (the profile names it), not at the bottom.

## Wall-skill set formats
- Turn-density sets: choose rep distances that maximize walls per meter — many 2-length
  reps beat few long reps when the wall is the target. A single-length rep has NO
  mid-rep turn: skill-integration reps must be 2+ lengths.
- Approach-only reps: start mid-pool, swim ~15m in at target speed, turn, break out,
  stop — full turn quality at a fraction of the swimming cost. High reps, fresh state.
- Tired-wall counts: late in a session, count clean walls out of N on a moderate rep —
  the measurable version of "turns hold up under fatigue."
- Execution-standard overlays on ordinary sets: "every wall counts" — the aerobic set
  carries the turn standard as its task (set-design.md task sets), so integration is
  trained without dedicating the whole session to it.

## Open turns (when programmed)
Two-hand or one-hand touch (stroke-legal), knees to chest, feet plant as the hand
leaves, push on the side rotating to front. Coach the same push-off/breakout standards
— the wall leave is identical currency in every turn.

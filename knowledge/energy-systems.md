# Energy systems & training zones — the engine map

Reference for the Physiologist (primary) and Coach. GENERIC method — swimmer-specific
paces, capacities, and constraints live in memory/ and always win on conflict.

## The three engines
Every swim is powered by a blend of three systems; the blend is set by effort and duration.
- ALACTIC (ATP-PCr): 0-10s of maximal power. No lactate, no burn — just runs out. Refills
  ~50% in 30s, ~full in 2-3 min of easy recovery. Trains: pure speed, power, starts,
  push-offs. Requires FULL recovery between efforts or it silently becomes glycolytic work.
- GLYCOLYTIC (anaerobic): ~10s-2min of hard effort. Produces lactate + the burn; expensive
  to recover from (clearance 20-60 min, faster with easy swimming). Trains: lactate
  production and tolerance, race-pace speed endurance. Small doses transform a session;
  large doses wreck the next one.
- AEROBIC: everything longer, and the recovery engine between all efforts. Adapts slowest
  (weeks-months) but carries the most total capacity. The base every other quality sits on.

## Zones, anchored to CSS
CSS (critical swim speed) = the fastest pace sustainable without accumulating lactate —
the aerobic/threshold boundary. Estimate from two honest timed efforts, one short one long
(protocol in pace-and-time.md). All zones are paces relative to CSS, not feelings.

| Zone | What it is | Pace vs CSS | Work:rest | Reps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 recovery/technique | no metabolic stress; skill lives here | much slower | generous | any |
| Z2 aerobic base | "strong, not slow"; the volume workhorse | ~CSS +4-8s/100 | ~1:0.1 (5-15s/rep) | long |
| Z3 threshold | at/just under CSS; hurts late, sustainable | ~CSS | 1:0.1-1:0.3 (SHORT rest) | mid-long |
| Z4 VO2max / aerobic power | above CSS; lactate climbs, VO2 maxes | CSS −2-5s/100 | 1:0.5-1:1 | 30s-2min |
| Z5 speed / lactate | race pace and faster | max or near | 1:4-1:8 (LONG rest) | 10-40s |

- REST DEFINES THE ZONE. Same distance and pace with different rest is a different
  adaptation: threshold with generous rest is not threshold; sprints with short rest are
  not sprints (they turn glycolytic and slow). Choose the rest first, then the send-off.
- Z4 is the costliest per meter — cap it at 1-2 exposures/week even for well-recovered
  athletes. Z5 alactic speed is cheap IF rest is honest (full recovery); Z5 lactate
  tolerance is the most expensive work in swimming — rare, deliberate doses.
- The ultra-short race-pace model (many single- or double-length reps at target race pace,
  brief rest, STOP at first failed rep) trains race pace with tolerable fatigue and intact
  technique — the useful middle ground between Z3 grind and Z5 heroics.

## Recovery kinetics — what rest actually buys
- 10-20s: partial PCr refill; enough to hold Z2/Z3 quality, not speed.
- 45s-1min: substantial PCr refill; enough for strong Z4 reps.
- 2-3+ min: near-full alactic recovery; required for true max speed to be max.
- Easy swimming clears lactate ~2x faster than hanging on the wall — active recovery
  between hard blocks is functional, not filler.
- Breath-restricted work adds a hidden recovery cost the clock doesn't show (CO2 debt);
  it always needs more rest than its plain equivalent (see breath-underwater.md).

## Sequencing a session — interference rules
1. Skill/technique before fatigue, always: a tired nervous system grooves bad patterns.
2. Pure speed (Z5 alactic) needs freshness — early, after warmup, or as a short touch
   late ONLY if the goal is speed-under-fatigue and quality is monitored per rep.
3. Threshold/VO2 work sits mid-session; aerobic Z2 fits anywhere; Z1 closes.
4. Distribute intensity: several purposeful blocks at real (varied) difficulty beat one
   isolated hard block padded with junk-easy filler. Every block earns its place.
5. Pre-fatigue is a TOOL, not an accident: when the target quality must work under
   fatigue (e.g. holding technique or breath control late), engineer honest aerobic load
   BEFORE the target block — deliberately, at a dose the athlete recovers from.

## The low-volume athlete (few sessions/week, every-other-day or sparser)
- Full recovery between sessions is the default, so a high hard-fraction is sustainable;
  no fixed easy:hard ratio applies. Manage LOAD (rpe × duration) and RECOVERY EVIDENCE
  instead (the Physiologist's decision order).
- The aerobic base still rules: at low volume, every aerobic meter must be purposeful —
  broken/short-rest accumulation at honest pace builds base as well as continuous swims,
  with better technique retention.
- Adaptation timescales for expectations: neuromuscular skill = days-weeks; CO2 tolerance
  = days-weeks (fast); glycolytic power = weeks; aerobic capacity = weeks-months. Don't
  chase week-to-week aerobic jumps; do expect fast early skill/breath-control gains.
